# Part 2 — A Day in the Life

I am the backend engineer on the OmniSense Dashboard team. Every morning at 9:00 AM, we begin with a 15-minute standup led by Emilio, our Scrum Master. Each person answers three questions: what they completed yesterday, what they plan to work on today, and whether they have any blockers.

After standup, Daniel shares a few customer updates from the latest pilot deployment. Michael follows up by refining a few user stories based on customer feedback, while Trista reviews new dashboard mockups with Manjesh before frontend implementation begins.

I spend the morning working on a new telemetry ingestion API that receives data from flow meters and stores it in our cloud database. When I have questions about the database schema, I work with Kevin to make sure the tables and indexes support high-volume writes efficiently.

Around lunchtime, Pence and I discuss whether a new reporting feature should be implemented through REST polling or WebSockets. Since Pence is our system architect, we review scalability, maintenance, and customer requirements before agreeing on the final design.

During the afternoon, Manjesh integrates my backend APIs into the React dashboard while Wei begins testing the completed features in our staging environment. She reports a bug through Jira after discovering that one of the dashboard charts occasionally displays duplicate telemetry records.

Jeffrey automatically deploys every merged pull request to the staging environment using our GitHub Actions CI/CD pipeline running on AWS. When Wei confirms the bug, I reproduce the issue locally, fix it, submit a pull request, and ask Pence to review the backend changes. After approval, Jeffrey deploys the updated build to staging, and Wei verifies the fix before marking the story complete.

Before ending the day, Emilio checks our sprint progress and updates the burndown chart. Daniel reviews completed work with the customer and adjusts priorities for upcoming stories. Everyone leaves knowing exactly what will be worked on tomorrow.

# Part 3 — Interview Q&A

### 1. Pick a product first — one sentence, what are you building and who is it for?

My team is building the OmniSense Dashboard, a real-time oil and gas monitoring platform that helps field operators and production managers monitor flow meter data and make operational decisions.

### 2. How many people are on your team and why?

My team has ten people because it gives us enough specialized roles to build, test, deploy, and support a production software system while keeping communication manageable.

### 3. List every team member — name, title, and one-line responsibility.

Daniel Kwan is our Product Owner and writes the PRD while setting product priorities. Emilio Rojas is our Scrum Master and Tech Lead who facilitates Agile ceremonies and guides technical decisions. Trista Tu is our UX/UI Designer responsible for user experience and interface design. Michael Nguyen is our Business Analyst who translates business needs into user stories. Kevin Jung is our Database Administrator who manages our database architecture and performance. Wei Yan is our QA Engineer responsible for testing and release validation. Jeffrey Cherry is our DevOps Engineer who manages AWS infrastructure and CI/CD. I am Suzy Chen, the Backend Engineer responsible for APIs and backend services. Manjesh Prasad is our Frontend Engineer who builds the React application. Pence Chhay is our Full Stack Engineer and System Architect who designs the overall technical architecture.

### 4. Who is your Product Owner and what do they do day to day?

Our Product Owner is Daniel Kwan. Every day he meets with customers, gathers feedback, updates the product roadmap, prioritizes the backlog, and writes or refines the Product Requirement Document.

### 5. Who is your Scrum Master and how are they different from a project manager?

Our Scrum Master is Emilio Rojas. Unlike a traditional project manager, he doesn't assign tasks or manage people. Instead, he facilitates Agile ceremonies, removes blockers, improves team processes, and helps the team stay productive.

### 6. How many engineers do you have and how are they split?

We have five engineers. I handle backend development, Manjesh focuses on frontend development, Pence works as a full stack engineer and architect, Jeffrey focuses on DevOps, and Kevin specializes in database engineering and administration.

### 7. Do you have a QA engineer?

Yes. Wei Yan is our QA engineer, but quality is everyone's responsibility. Developers write unit tests and integration tests, while QA performs end-to-end testing and release validation.

### 8. Who handles deployments and infrastructure?

Jeffrey Cherry owns deployments, AWS infrastructure, CI/CD pipelines, monitoring, and production releases.

### 9. Do you have a designer?

Yes. Trista Tu works closely with Daniel and Michael before development starts. During the sprint she collaborates with frontend engineering to answer design questions and review implementation.

### 10. How long are your sprints?

We use two-week sprints because they provide enough time to complete meaningful features while allowing us to respond quickly to customer feedback.

### 11. Walk me through Sprint Planning.

During Sprint Planning, Daniel presents the highest-priority backlog items, Michael explains the business requirements, the engineers estimate the work, and the team commits to a realistic amount of work for the next two weeks.

### 12. What does your Daily Standup look like?

Our standup lasts about fifteen minutes. Emilio runs the meeting, and everyone answers what they completed yesterday, what they will do today, and whether they have any blockers.

### 13. What is a Sprint Review?

At the end of the sprint, we demonstrate completed features to Daniel, stakeholders, and customers. We collect feedback that helps shape future backlog priorities.

### 14. What is a Retrospective?

The retrospective is an internal team meeting where we discuss what went well, what didn't, and what improvements we want to make during the next sprint.

### 15. What is Backlog Refinement?

Backlog refinement happens once a week. Daniel, Michael, Emilio, and the engineering team review upcoming stories, clarify requirements, estimate complexity, and prepare work for future sprints.

### 16. What is your Definition of Done?

A story is done only after the code is reviewed, tests pass, QA verifies the feature, documentation is updated, and the feature is successfully deployed to staging without critical issues.

### 17. You found a critical bug two days before release.

I immediately tell Emilio and Daniel, create a high-priority Jira ticket, notify Wei to reproduce the issue, work with the team to fix it, and delay the release if the bug affects customers.

### 18. A stakeholder wants a new feature mid-sprint.

Daniel evaluates the request. If it is not urgent, it goes into the product backlog for a future sprint. If it is business-critical, Daniel works with the team to adjust sprint scope before adding it.

### 19. You are blocked on a ticket.

I first ask the teammate who can help, such as Kevin for database issues or Pence for architecture questions. If I remain blocked, I bring it up during standup or ask Emilio to help remove the blocker.

### 20. Two engineers disagree on a technical approach.

They discuss the trade-offs and review technical evidence. If they cannot reach agreement, Pence makes the architectural recommendation after considering scalability, maintainability, and business goals.

### 21. The designer is out sick.

We use the approved Figma designs and existing design system. If clarification is needed, Michael and Daniel help answer product questions until Trista returns.

### 22. Your deployment pipeline breaks.

Jeffrey takes ownership because he manages our CI/CD pipeline, while the engineering team works with him to identify and resolve the root cause.

### 23. What is a Product Backlog?

The Product Backlog is the complete prioritized list of future work. Daniel owns it and continuously updates priorities based on customer feedback.

### 24. What is a Sprint Backlog?

The Sprint Backlog contains the user stories selected during Sprint Planning. It is created collaboratively by the Product Owner and the development team.

### 25. When do you write a technical design document?

We write a technical design document before implementing large features or architectural changes. Pence reviews the architecture, and Emilio approves the implementation plan with input from the engineering team.

### 26. How does your team file and track bugs?

We track bugs in Jira. QA creates bug tickets, developers update their progress, and the Scrum Master monitors priority and resolution.

### 27. Where does your team write things down?

We use Confluence for documentation, Jira for project tracking, GitHub for source control and pull requests, Figma for design, Slack for communication, and AWS CloudWatch for monitoring.

### 28. How does your team decide what goes into the next sprint?

Daniel prioritizes the backlog based on customer value and business goals. During Sprint Planning, the team estimates capacity and commits only to work we believe we can complete.

### 29. What happens if your team consistently doesn't finish sprint work?

During the retrospective we analyze why the work is not being completed. We improve estimation, reduce sprint scope if necessary, and remove recurring blockers before the next sprint.

### 30. How does a brand new feature go from idea to production?

A customer request first goes to Daniel, who works with Michael to define the business requirements. Trista designs the user experience, the engineering team creates technical designs, developers implement the feature, QA tests it, Jeffrey deploys it through our CI/CD pipeline, and after final approval it is released to production.

### 31. If someone asked you in an interview "Describe your team."

My team is a cross-functional Agile team of ten people building a real-time cloud dashboard for the oil and gas industry. We have dedicated product, design, business analysis, development, database, QA, and DevOps roles. We work in two-week sprints, use Jira and Confluence to manage work, GitHub for collaboration, and AWS for deployment. Everyone owns quality, communication is very open, and we work closely together to deliver customer value every sprint.
